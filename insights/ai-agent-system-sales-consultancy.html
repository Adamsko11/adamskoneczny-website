I Built a 28-Agent AI System to Run My Sales Consultancy. Here Is What Actually Works.

How a 3-person B2B sales consultancy uses 28 specialized AI agents across sales, marketing, operations, and client delivery to operate like a company 10x its size.

Adam Skoneczny Published April 4, 2026 Updated April 6, 2026 Fresh

The Problem: Revenue Without Scale

Three years ago, EVIT was a consultancy with a real problem: we could deliver great results for B2B SaaS companies in Southeast Asia trying to enter European and US markets. But we couldn't deliver at scale. It was just me, one business developer, and one delivery person. We were capped at maybe 3-4 active clients at any given time, which meant capped revenue.

We had two options:

  1. Hire. Expand to 15-20 people, carry that fixed cost, hope to fill seats.
  2. Automate. Build AI agents to handle specific functions that don't require human judgment—lead research, outreach, content, follow-up qualification.

We chose option 2. Not because hiring is bad, but because hiring would move us away from what we're actually good at: selling, strategic thinking, and client outcomes.

Key Insight

Scaling via AI agents is not about replacing people—it's about multiplying the leverage of the people you have. 28 agents don't do the job of 28 employees. They do the job of 15-20 support roles, freeing the three of us to focus on strategy, client relationships, and revenue decisions.

How We Structured the System

Our 28 agents are organized into five functional groups:

Revenue Engine 5 Lead generation, qualification, outreach, pipeline management Qualified opportunities for sales calls
Marketing 7 Strategy, content creation, SEO, design, ads, daily ops Published content, campaigns, brand presence
Operations 3 Admin, accounting, coordination Financial records, schedules, workflows

The Math: Cost vs. Capacity

Our entire agent system—all 28 agents, running autonomously, loading context files, updating pipelines—costs about $300 per month in tooling.

GEO-Optimization Note

For B2B consultancies targeting specific geographies (we focus on APAC entering Europe/US), use agents specialized in location-based filtering. Our GEO agent cross-references company location, founder location, market entry patterns, and regulatory complexity to prioritize high-intent, near-term deals.

Agent Design Principles We Learned

1. Clear Job Descriptions

Not just "write content." Specifically: "Write thought leadership articles in Adam's voice for LinkedIn, targeting B2B SaaS founders. 1,200-1,500 words. One article per week. Post on Mondays at 8 AM UTC."

2. Context Files Are Everything

Every agent loads: Voice Guide, ICP Document, Playbooks, Recent Work, Decision Frameworks. Agents with loaded context make better decisions.

3. Guard Rails Prevent Disasters

Every agent has rules for what it can never do: Never invent metrics. Never publish without review. Never claim unverified results.

4. Quality Standards Matter

Each agent has explicit criteria: Content > 90 grammar score. Scout > ICP match >= 75. Outreach > Personalization >= 8/10.

5. Escalation Rules Save Time

If confidence < 60%: Flag for review. If breaks guardrail: Flag immediately. If high-value opportunity: Flag for CEO prioritization.

The Real ROI

Speed. Consistency. Scalability without hiring. Focus. Learning. This is how you operate like a 30-person organization with three people.

FAQs

How long does it take to build?

One agent: 1-2 focused days. Full 28-agent system: 3 months. Start with highest-leverage. Iterate weekly. Build next agent. Compound effect over months is exponential.

What if an agent makes a mistake?

It flags it (if designed well). You review and correct. That feedback loops back into context so it learns.

Can I use this for my industry?

Yes. Replace "B2B sales" with your function. The framework works for marketing, product, operations, finance—any domain with clear inputs, decision rules, and quality standards.

What's the catch?

Time investment upfront to write agent specs. Then ongoing refinement. But that's an investment in clarity, not in hiring.
